\chapter{Simulation setup and implementation}
\label{ch:simulation}

This chapter describes the construction of the lensed 
BBH population used as input to the PE pipeline. The simulation proceeds in two stages: first, a large Monte Carlo (MC) population of strongly lensed BBH events is generated with \textsc{LeR} v0.4.2 
\cite{phurailatpam2026lerlvkligovirgokagracollaboration}, and then a realistic detectability filter is applied to select the subset of events that would be observed by the H1 and L1 detectors under O5 sensitivity conditions.

\section{Lensed BBH population with \textsc{LeR}}
\label{sec:LER_population}

\textsc{LeR} (LVK Event Rate calculator) is a 
Python framework for the joint simulation and rate 
computation of unlensed and strongly lensed GW 
populations \cite{phurailatpam2026lerlvkligovirgokagracollaboration}. It integrates source and lens population sampling, lens equation solving via \textsc{lenstronomy} \cite{lenstronomy}, and detector selection into a single reproducible pipeline. All lensing calculations are performed in the geometric optics regime and under the thin lens approximation, both of which are valid for galaxy-scale lenses in the LIGO frequency band (see \S\ref{sec:lensing_intro}).

The simulation is initialized with the following 
configuration, specifying the detector network, 
noise curves, and waveform approximant:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=numpyStyle]
ler = LeR(
    npool=6,
    event_type="BBH",
    ifos=['L1', 'H1'],
    psds={'L1': 'T2500310-v2_O5a_strain_psd.txt',
          'H1': 'T2500310-v2_O5a_strain_psd.txt'},
    waveform_approximant="IMRPhenomXPHM",
    spin_zero=False,
    spin_precession=True,
)
\end{lstlisting}

Here, \texttt{npool} sets the number of parallel worker 
processes used for the MC sampling and SNR 
calculations. The simulation was run in a Jupyter 
notebook on the CIT JupyterHub, using the \texttt{igwn} 
conda environment (Python 3.11.14) distributed via 
CVMFS, with 48 CPU cores available. The \texttt{event\_type="BBH"} argument selects the BBH source population model described in Table~\ref{tab:LER_params}, as opposed to the BNS or NS-BH options also supported by \textsc{LeR}. The \texttt{ifos} argument specifies the detector network over which the network SNR $\rho_\mathrm{net}$ is computed, here restricted to the two LIGO detectors, H1 and L1.

The \texttt{psds} argument sets the noise curve 
assigned to each detector, here the O5a design sensitivity PSD \cite{O5PSD}. This choice is motivated by the goal of simulating a realistic future observing scenario. The default PSD in \textsc{LeR} approximates O4 sensitivity, but no strongly lensed events were detected in O4. Simulating at O5a sensitivity instead, the first epoch of the O5 run with a BNS range of 225\,Mpc \cite{O5PSD}, provides a more informative and forward-looking setting for this study. The 
\texttt{waveform\_approximant} argument selects 
\texttt{IMRPhenomXPHM} \cite{IMRPhenomXPHM} for the SNR 
computation, which incorporates spin precession and 
higher-order multipole moments. Finally, \texttt{spin\_zero=False} enables non-zero component spins in the source population, and \texttt{spin\_precession=True} allows for spin tilts misaligned with the orbital angular momentum, so that the resulting waveforms include precession effects. Both are necessary for the spin parameters listed in Table~\ref{tab:LER_params} to be sampled rather than fixed to zero.

The population is generated by drawing events in 
batches of 20\,000 until 500 strongly lensed 
detectable events are collected, each requiring 
that at least two images independently satisfy 
$\rho_\mathrm{net} \geq 10$:

\newpage
\begin{lstlisting}[style=numpyStyle]
lensed_params = ler.selecting_n_lensed_detectable_events(
    size=500,
    batch_size=20000,
    snr_threshold=10,
    num_img=2,
    resume=False,
    output_jsonfile="n_lensed_params_bbh.txt",
)
\end{lstlisting}

The SNR threshold of $\rho_\mathrm{net} \geq 10$ and 
the requirement of at least two detectable images follow standard detection criteria adopted in GW searches, so that the resulting population reflects events that a real detector network could plausibly identify and confidently claim as lensed, rather than a theoretical population extending down to marginal or sub-threshold significance.

A total of approximately 152\,000 candidate events 
were drawn to collect the 500 detectable ones, 
yielding a total lensed event rate of 
$\mathcal{R}_L \approx 0.75\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ 
for this detector configuration and sensitivity. 
This value is consistent with early forecasts for 
second-generation detector networks at design-like 
sensitivity, which span roughly $0.1$-$10\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ 
depending on the assumed high-redshift merger rate and 
detector network \cite{Wierda_2021}, and is somewhat lower than forecasts for the full three- or four-detector network, reflecting the more limited H1+L1 configuration adopted here.


\section{Injected parameter distributions}
\label{sec:injected_params}

The 500 events produced by \textsc{LeR} represent the strongly lensed population detectable under ideal observing conditions. To obtain a more realistic injection set, a duty cycle filter is applied: for each image of each event, both H1 and L1 are independently activated with probability 
$p_\mathrm{det} = 0.70$, reflecting typical O4/O5 
single-detector duty cycles \cite{GWTC5}. An image is 
retained only if both detectors are simultaneously active and $\rho_\mathrm{net} \geq 10$. Events with fewer than two surviving images are discarded; when more than two images survive, only the two with the highest network SNR are kept. After applying this filter once to the fixed 500-event 
population, 157 events are selected as the final injection set analyzed with \textsc{bilby}.

Figures~\ref{fig:snr_hist}--\ref{fig:timedelay_hist} 
show the distributions of the key parameters for 
the 157 injected events. The network SNR 
distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:snr_hist}) peaks 
just above the threshold of 10, as expected for 
a population dominated by threshold-crossing 
events, with a tail extending to higher values 
for strongly magnified or favorably oriented 
systems. The primary source-frame mass distribution 
(Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_hist}) reflects the underlying 
LIGO O3 Power Law + Gaussian model, with a 
concentration at low masses and a secondary 
feature near $30\,M_\odot$. The joint 
$m_1^{\mathrm{src}}$--$m_2^{\mathrm{src}}$ 
distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_scatter}) shows 
that most events lie close to $q\sim1$, and that 
the highest-SNR events are concentrated around 
total masses of $50$--$70\,M_\odot$, where the 
detector network is most sensitive. The 
magnification distribution 
(Fig.~\ref{fig:magnification_hist}) 
shows a sharp peak near zero with extended tails, 
where negative values correspond to Type II images 
with mirror-inverted parity (see 
\S\ref{sec:strong_lensing}). The time delay 
distribution between the two images 
(Fig.~\ref{fig:timedelay_hist}) spans from 
minutes to months, consistent with galaxy-scale 
lenses in the geometric optics regime 
(\S\ref{sec:timedelay}).
