\chapter{Parameters of Compact Binary Coalescences}
\label{Parameters_CBC_appendix}

This appendix provides a reference summary of the parameters used to describe a CBC system with quasi-circular orbits, following the conventions of \cite{parameters_CBC, GWTC4_introduction}. The parameters are grouped into mass, spin, tidal, and extrinsic categories; those employed in the injections and parameter estimation analyses of this work are indicated with $\star$.

\multicolumn{3}{l}{\textit{\textbf{Mass parameters}}} \\
\hline
\addlinespace[5pt]

\, Primary/secondary mass & $m_1,\,m_2^\star$ &
Mass of the more (less) massive component, $m_1\geq m_2$ $[M_\odot]$ \\

\, Chirp mass & $\mathcal{M}$ &
$\mathcal{M}=\eta^{3/5}M$ $[M_\odot]$ \\

\, Total mass & $M$ &
$M=m_1+m_2$ $[M_\odot]$ \\

\, Final mass & $M_f$ &
Mass of the remnant $[M_\odot]$ \\

\, Mass ratio & $q$ &
$q=m_2/m_1\leq 1$ [dimensionless] \\

\, Symmetric mass ratio & $\eta$ &
$\eta=m_1m_2/M^2\leq 1/4$ [dimensionless] \\

\, Radiated energy & $E_\mathrm{rad}$ &
$E_\mathrm{rad}=(M-M_f)c^2$ [energy] \\

%------------------------------------------------
\addlinespace[5pt]
\multicolumn{3}{l}{\textit{\textbf{Spin parameters}}} \\
\hline
\addlinespace[5pt]

\, Primary/secondary spin vectors & $\mathbf{S}_1,\,\mathbf{S}_2$ &
Spin angular momentum of the primary/secondary [ang. mom.] \\

\, Dimensionless spin magnitudes & $\chi_1,\,\chi_2^\star$ &
$\chi_{1,2}=c|\mathbf{S}_{1,2}|/(Gm_{1,2}^2)\leq 1$ [dimensionless] \\

\, Remnant spin magnitude & $\chi_f$ &
$\chi_f=cS_f/(GM_f^2)\leq 1$  where $S_f$ is the magnitude of the remnant's spin
angular momentum [dimensionless] \\

\, Orbital ang. momentum & $\mathbf{L}$ &
Instantaneous orbital angular momentum about the center of mass [ang. mom.] \\

\, Total ang. momentum & $\mathbf{J}$ &
$\mathbf{J}=\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{S}_1+\mathbf{S}_2$ [ang. mom.] \\

\, Primary/secondary tilt angles & $\theta_1,\,\theta_2^\star$ &
Angle between $\mathbf{S}_{1,2}$ and $\mathbf{L}$ [angle] \\

\, Spin azimuthal difference & $\phi_{12}^\star$ &
Angle between $\mathbf{L}\times(\mathbf{S}_1\times\mathbf{L})$ and $\mathbf{L}\times(\mathbf{S}_2\times\mathbf{L})$ [angle] \\

\, Effective inspiral spin & $\chi_\mathrm{eff}$ &
$(m_1\chi_{1z}+m_2\chi_{2z})/M$ [dimensionless] \\

\, Effective precession spin & $\chi_p$ &
Avg.\ over precession cycles (see Eq.~(16) of \cite{GWTC5}) [dimensionless] \\

%------------------------------------------------
\multicolumn{3}{l}{\textit{\textbf{Tidal parameters}}} \\
\hline
\addlinespace[5pt]

\, Tidal deformabilities & $\Lambda_1,\,\Lambda_2$ &
Dimensionless tidal deformability; $\Lambda_{1,2}=0$ for BHs [dimensionless] \\

\, Effective tidal deformability & $\tilde{\Lambda}$ &
Dominant tidal combination; $\tilde{\Lambda}=0$ for BBH [dimensionless] \\

%------------------------------------------------
\multicolumn{3}{l}{\textit{\textbf{Extrinsic parameters}}} \\
\hline
\addlinespace[5pt]

\, Luminosity distance & $D_L^\star$ &
Distance to the source [Mpc] \\

\, Redshift & $z$ &
Computable from $D_L$ assuming a cosmological model [dimensionless] \\

\, Source inclination & $\theta_{JN}^\star$ &
Angle between $\mathbf{J}$ and line of sight $\mathbf{N}$ [angle] \\

\, Orbital inclination & $\iota$ &
Angle between $\mathbf{L}$ and $\mathbf{N}$ [angle] \\

\, Coalescence time & $t_c^\star$ &
GPS time at the geocenter [time] \\

\, Coalescence phase & $\phi_c^\star$ &
Orbital phase at coalescence [angle] \\

\, Right ascension & $\alpha^\star$ &
Celestial longitude [angle] \\

\, Declination & $\delta^\star$ &
Celestial latitude [angle] \\

\, Polarization angle & $\psi^\star$ &
Rotates polarizations in the plane orthogonal to propagation [angle] \\

\, Azimuthal spin angle & $\phi_{JL}^\star$ &
Angle between $\mathbf{J}$ and $\mathbf{L}$ projected on the sky plane [angle] \\
\end{longtable}

\renewcommand{\thechapter}{B} 
\chapter{Reproducibility tutorial. Linux environment and supercomputing tasks.}
\label{Reproducibility tutorial}


The motivation for including this section comes from the desire to make life easier for future students who will use the same tools, especially since many parts of this tutorial came with their fair share of headaches.

This appendix provides a detailed, step-by-step overview of the workflow followed to run my experiments on a Linux-based high-performance computing (HPC) system. The goal is to ensure full reproducibility and transparency. The system used was Picasso, which uses a Linux environment and the SLURM workload manager.

\section*{Accessing the Supercomputer}
Access to the HPC cluster was provided via secure shell (SSH). The connection was established using:

\begin{verbatim}
ssh username@cluster.domain.edu
\end{verbatim}

For convenience, an SSH configuration file was set up:

\begin{verbatim}
# ~/.ssh/config
Host mycluster
    HostName cluster.domain.edu
    User myusername
    IdentityFile ~/.ssh/id_rsa
\end{verbatim}

\subsection*{3. File and Directory Organization}
The working directory was structured as follows:

\begin{verbatim}
/home/username/
 project/
  data/
   scripts/
    results/
   logs/
\end{verbatim}

This structure helped maintain organization and simplified automation.

\subsection*{4. Working Environment}
The HPC system used a module system to load required software. The Python environment was prepared as follows:

\begin{verbatim}
module load python/3.10
python -m venv env
source env/bin/activate
\end{verbatim}

Additional Python packages were installed using \texttt{pip} or \texttt{requirements.txt}.

\subsection*{5. Key Commands and Utilities}
Here is a summary of the most commonly used commands:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|l|p{10cm}|}
\hline
\textbf{Command} & \textbf{Description} \\
\hline
\texttt{ssh} & Secure connection to the HPC server. \\
\texttt{scp} & Copy files between local and remote systems. \\
\texttt{sbatch} & Submit job scripts to SLURM. \\
\texttt{squeue} & View active jobs in the queue. \\
\texttt{ls} & List files and directories. \\
\texttt{ll} & Detailed list of files (usually alias for ls -l). \\
\texttt{cd} & Change directory. \\
\texttt{pwd} & Show current working directory. \\
\texttt{mkdir} & Create a new directory. \\
\texttt{rm} & Remove files or directories. \\
\texttt{cp} & Copy files or directories. \\
\texttt{mv} & Move or rename files or directories. \\
\texttt{less} & View file contents page by page. \\
\texttt{tail} & Display the last lines of a file (useful for logs). \\
\texttt{head} & Display the first lines of a file. \\
\texttt{grep} & Search for patterns in files. \\
\texttt{nano} & Simple terminal text editor. \\
\texttt{df} & Show disk space usage. \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection*{6. Job Submission and SLURM Scripts}
Jobs were submitted to the scheduler using SLURM. Below is a sample job script:

\begin{verbatim}
#!/bin/bash
#SBATCH --job-name=my_job
#SBATCH --output=logs/output_%j.txt
#SBATCH --error=logs/error_%j.txt
#SBATCH --time=01:00:00
#SBATCH --mem=4G
#SBATCH --cpus-per-task=2

module load python/3.10
source env/bin/activate
python scripts/my_script.py
\end{verbatim}

To submit this job:
\begin{verbatim}
sbatch job.sh
\end{verbatim}

To check job status:
\begin{verbatim}
squeue -u myusername
\end{verbatim}

\subsection*{7. Running the Experiments}
Experiments were launched using prepared scripts with configurable arguments. For example:

\begin{verbatim}
python train_model.py --dataset data/input.csv --epochs 50 --lr 0.001
\end{verbatim}

Each run was logged using `tee` and redirected output:

\begin{verbatim}
python script.py > logs/run1.out 2>&1
\end{verbatim}

\subsection*{8. Troubleshooting and Common Issues}
Typical problems encountered included:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Module conflicts:} Solved by purging modules with \texttt{module purge}.
  \item \textbf{Permission errors:} Solved by adjusting file permissions via \texttt{chmod}.
  \item \textbf{Job hangs:} Resolved by checking SLURM node load and memory limits.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{9. Optimization and Performance Tuning (Optional)}
Where relevant, job parameters were optimized to use resources efficiently. For example:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Multi-threading enabled via \texttt{--cpus-per-task}.
  \item Memory profiling using \texttt{/usr/bin/time -v}.
  \item Parallel jobs launched via SLURM job arrays.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{10. Additional Resources}
\begin{itemize}
  \item SLURM documentation: \url{https://slurm.schedmd.com/documentation.html}
  \item SSH key setup: \url{https://www.ssh.com/academy/ssh/keygen}
  \item GitHub repository: \url{https://github.com/XxavierGarcia/TFM_reproducibility_material}
\end{itemize}
