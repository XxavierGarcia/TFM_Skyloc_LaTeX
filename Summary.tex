The structure of this thesis is organized as follows:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1cm]

  \item[\chapterlink{Introduction_to_General_Relativity}{Chapter 1: Introduction to general relativity.}] Develops the theoretical foundations of GR, from the equivalence principle and the mathematics of curved spacetime to the Einstein field equations and the Schwarzschild solution.

  \item[\chapterlink{Gravitational-wave theory}{Chapter 2: 
  Gravitational wave theory.}] Derives gravitational waves from the linearized field equations, characterizes their polarization states and effect on matter, and introduces the detection principle and Bayesian parameter estimation framework.

  \item[\chapterlink{ch:lensing}{Chapter 3: Gravitational lensing of gravitational waves.}] Develops the strong lensing formalism and characterizes the signatures lensing imprints on gravitational wave signals, motivating the joint analysis of lensed image pairs for sky localization.

  \item[\chapterlink{Simulation_setup}{Chapter 4: Simulation setup and implementation.}] \textit{To be completed.}

  \textit{To be completed.}

  \item[\chapterlink{Parameters_CBC_appendix}{Appendix A: Parameters of compact binary coalescences.}] Complete description of the parameter space of precessing binary black hole systems.

  Reproducibility tutorial.}] Steps required to reproduce the results presented in this work.

\end{description}
